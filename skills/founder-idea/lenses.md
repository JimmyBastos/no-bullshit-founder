# Three lenses for the pre-mortem

Short summaries, in our words, of three published ideas. Use them as
checklists. Never speak as the authors or claim they endorse anything.

## Offer value

Source: Alex Hormozi, *$100M Offers* (2021).

- **The market comes first.** A strong offer fails in a weak market. Check
  four things: the pain is big, the buyers have money, they are easy to reach,
  and the market is growing.
- **The value equation.** Buyers value an offer more when the outcome is
  bigger and more likely, and less when it takes long or asks a lot of effort.
  Most offers fail on likelihood (no proof) or effort (too much work for the
  buyer).
- **Don't compete only on price.** If being cheaper is the only difference, it
  is a commodity.

Ask: Who has this pain and the money to fix it? Why would they believe it
works? How fast do they see a result?

## Defensibility

Source: Peter Thiel with Blake Masters, *Zero to One* (2014).

- **Competition eats margins.** Look for a small market you can lead first.
- **What lasts:** something much better than the next option on what matters,
  network effects, economies of scale, or a brand. Most small businesses have
  none of these at the start, so ask which one this could build.
- **Distribution is half of it.** A good product nobody can reach is not a
  business. Ask how the first 100 customers arrive and what each costs.

Ask: What small market can this lead? Why now? What stops a copycat in six
months?

## Focus

Source: Walter Isaacson, *Steve Jobs* (2011).

- **Focus means saying no.** Cut until what is left is the one thing the
  business must do well.
- **Start from the customer's experience** and work back to what has to be
  true to deliver it.
- **Every touchpoint is the product,** from the first ad to the follow-up.
  Problems hide where work is handed from one person or system to another.

Ask: What is the one thing? What would you cut? Where does the experience
break?
